# Kurometry

### Measuring Linear A

Alberto Acedo, PhD, independent researcher
Version 1.0, 16 September 2026

A book in four parts, 94 pages, with every figure measured against an explicit null model.

KU-RO is the Minoan word for "total". This book counts. It does not decipher Linear A. Not one phonetic value is fixed here that was not already known.

## What this work adds

Before the argument, the book lists which parts are new and which are the field's own restated, in four groups: new as method (the protocol, the decipherability function, a published account of an attempt that failed, and an appendix of every test run); new as measurement of what the field already holds without one (seven claims, from Packard and Lejeune to Younger and Kanta); new as measurement with no prior claim (the absence of vowel harmony with its power analysis, the syntax of the accounting document, the derivational morphology); and new as infrastructure (a distributional sign list, an index of 34,515 bibliographical entries, a generator of synthetic Minoan text, and thirty-four falsifiable predictions).

And it states what is not new, with the name of whoever holds it.

## The four parts

Part One — The corpus speaks about itself. What the Haghia Triada archive counts and how it seals; what the votive formula does. Description without judgement. Three of Schoep's claims hold in a narrower sense than stated; the entry groups of Packard and of Uchitel and Finkelberg are confirmed against a null for the first time; and the Minoan formula is aligned position by position against 1,357 real instances of the Egyptian *ḥtp-ḏj-nswt*.

Part Two — The attempt to read it. Thirteen candidate languages measured against one null: none reaches z = 3. And the reason the lexical criterion cannot discriminate: under the permissive phonology a CV syllabary imposes, fictitious grids reach 97.1%. Plus a proposal of our own, presented as the p = 0.0515 that selects it allows: candidates, not identifications.

Part Three — What it would take. What a reading proposal must declare; a protocol of ten minimum requirements, calibrated on four corpora, whose fourth no published proposal meets; a function that turns "we do not know whether this can be read" into a number; and the accounting of one day spent trying, which produced nothing and is published for that reason.

Part Four — Where this stands. The state of the question, with a verdict measured for sixteen claims the field currently holds, and seven walls named with their figures.

## The figure that recurs

Three independent routes agree on what is missing: 345 bits — 56 signs to anchor from outside, or some 788 further units of intact text.

The alignment model of Luo and colleagues (2021), extended here to syllabaries and validated on Linear B, takes off between 7 and 10 anchors of twenty signs; Linear A sits at 2.9. And the grid structure is present in the corpus but too weak to be recovered from it.

None of the seven walls is removed by computation. Five require excavation or specialist transcription; two require a find.

## The appendices

Appendix A lists every measurement: one hundred with a p-value, sixty-three below 0.05 and thirty-seven above; thirty-four avenues closed with the figure that closed them; twenty-four claims withdrawn with the reason. Rows that were withdrawn or that do not carry the reading first given them are marked and kept in place, because a list of surviving results only would be the selection this book argues against.

Appendix B is the map: what the discipline holds and who holds it, what was measured here, and the relation between the two, marked as confirmed, narrowed, refuted, disputed or not yet done. It exists because sixteen apparent findings of this project turned out to be already published, each caught by a search of a few minutes, and a map of that kind would have caught them sooner.

## What is public

Corpus, code, null models and the manifest holding every figure: github.com/BiomeMakers/kuro

The manifest names, for each claim, the condition that would refute it, and records 32 closed avenues and 24 withdrawn claims — six of them positive results of this project that a control destroyed.
